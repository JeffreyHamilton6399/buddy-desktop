What “local” means here

Buddy is local-first: it runs entirely on your machine, stores your key in its own local data folder, and calls the AI provider directly. There is no Buddy server, no proxy and no account — nothing of yours passes through us, because there is no “us” in the path.

It is not fully private, and we won't pretend otherwise. The language model, text-to-speech and speech recognition all run on the z-ai provider's servers, so your messages and voice recordings are sent there to be processed.

About the wake word: this build detects speech locally, then sends short audio clips to the AI provider to check whether you said “Hey Buddy”. So while the wake word is on, brief clips of ambient sound leave your device whenever something is loud enough to look like speech. Turn it off from the tray and nothing is captured until you press the mic yourself.

First launch

These builds aren't code-signed, so your OS will warn you the first time. That's expected — here's how to get past it:

  • macOS: right-click the app and choose Open (don't double-click). If it says the app is damaged, run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Buddy.app
  • Windows: on the SmartScreen prompt, click More info → Run anyway.
  • Linux: mark the AppImage executable — chmod +x Buddy-*.AppImage

What's inside

  • The draggable always-on-top orb, with its position remembered between launches
  • A Siri-style chat panel with spoken replies and a mic button
  • “Hey Buddy” wake word, with a tray toggle to switch it off
  • A tray/menu-bar icon: open Buddy, toggle the wake word, quit
  • A loopback-only local server that holds your key and speaks to the provider
