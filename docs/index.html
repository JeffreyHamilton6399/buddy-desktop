An AI that lives on your desktop, not on someone's server.

A small orb that sits above your other windows. Say “Hey Buddy” and it listens straight away; talk over its answer and it stops. No API key, no account, no sign-in — it brings its own model, its own voice and its own ears, and keeps working with the network unplugged.

What it does

Answers without asking anyone

A model runs inside the app through llama.cpp. Thirteen are a click away, from 0.6 GB up, and the list says which ones your machine can actually hold. An Ollama you already run appears beside them.

Speaks, and stops when you do

Kokoro generates the speech on your own CPU, in one of 28 voices — not the robotic one your operating system ships with. Start talking over it and it stops to listen, the way a person would.

Answers to its name

Whisper listens for “Hey Buddy” on-device and replies at the orb — no window opens over your work. There is no greeting to sit through; it starts listening the moment it hears you.

Does things, if you let it

Open a page, run a search, write a file, delete one. Off until you switch it on, files stay inside folders you pick by hand, anything deleted goes to the recycle bin, and every action is written into the chat as it happens.

Where each part runs

Job Default If you go looking
Thinking Qwen 3, in the app 12 other models, Ollama, any API key
Speaking Kokoro, in the app your OS's voices, a cloud voice
Listening Whisper, in the app your own Whisper server, a cloud service, off
Your files no access at all folders you pick, and nowhere else
Your chats your disk never uploaded

Buddy has three jobs, and out of the box all three happen on your computer. You can move any of them to a cloud provider — paste a key from Anthropic, OpenAI, Groq and the rest, and Buddy works out whose it is. Nothing asks you to.

The exception is the first launch, which downloads the model, the voice and the ears — about two and a half gigabytes in total, once. After that you can unplug the network and Buddy carries on. Please do check. No telemetry, no account.

Point a job at a cloud provider and that job stops being private: those messages go to them to be processed. What stays true either way is that there is no Buddy server and no proxy — nothing of yours passes through us, because there is no “us” in the path. Your key is stored on your machine and sent only to the provider it belongs to. Setting listening to off means the microphone is never opened at all.

What it needs

Brain Download Memory while answering
MiniCPM5 1B smallest 0.6 GB about 1.2 GB
Qwen 3 4B default 2.3 GB about 3.5 GB
GPT-OSS 20B largest 10.8 GB about 14 GB

A 64-bit machine — macOS 11 or later, Windows 10 or later, or a modern Linux. Eight gigabytes of memory is comfortable for the default brain, and the model list inside the app greys out anything your machine cannot hold rather than letting you find out the hard way.

A graphics card is used if you have one and is not required. Where one is used the model sits in its memory, so a bigger brain than your card can hold is the one thing worth avoiding — that is the setting that makes everything else on the machine feel slow.

Buddy loads the brain when you actually speak to it and gives the memory back after ten minutes idle, so sitting in your tray costs a few hundred megabytes rather than several gigabytes. The voice adds 156 MB and the ears 39 MB, both one-time and both far smaller than the brain.

Questions

Does it need the internet?

Only for the first launch, which fetches the model, the voice and the ears — about two and a half gigabytes, once. After that you can unplug the network and everything still works. Buddy reaches out again only if you point one of its three jobs at a cloud provider, or ask it to open or search something.

What does it send about me?

Nothing. There is no telemetry, no account and no analytics, and there is no Buddy server for anything to pass through. Point a job at a cloud provider and those messages go to that provider — your key is stored on your machine and sent only to whoever issued it.

Is it really free?

Yes, and MIT licensed. There is no paid tier and nothing to sign up for. If you choose to use a cloud provider you pay them directly, at their prices, with your own key.

Will it slow my computer down?

Sitting in your tray it holds a few hundred megabytes. The brain is loaded when you speak to it and released after ten minutes of not being used, so the several gigabytes a language model needs are only spent while you are actually getting answers. The first question after a quiet stretch waits a few seconds for that load.

Can it touch my files?

Not until you switch it on. With the system switch on it can open pages, look at your screen and read, write and delete files — and it will still refuse programs, keys and databases, keep a .bak of anything it overwrites, send deletions to the recycle bin, and write every action into the chat as it happens. Naming folders narrows where it may write.

How do I remove it?

Uninstall it the way you would anything else, then delete Buddy's folder to reclaim the models — %APPDATA%\Buddy on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Buddy on macOS, ~/.config/Buddy on Linux. Your chats are plain files in there. Nothing is left anywhere else, because nothing was ever anywhere else.
