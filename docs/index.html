The things Buddy is built to do well.

  • ◎

    Always one click away

    A frameless, always-on-top orb you can drag anywhere. It remembers where you left it and stays out of your taskbar.

  • ◍

    Talks back to you

    Every reply is spoken in a warm voice with a live equaliser, and you can mute it with one tap when you'd rather just read.

  • ◌

    Hears “Hey Buddy”

    Leave the wake word on and Buddy answers to its name — panel open, a quick “Yeah?”, ready to listen. Toggle it off from the tray any time.

  • ◈

    Nothing to set up

    No API key, no account, no provider to pick. Buddy downloads its own language model once, then thinks on your machine with llama.cpp and talks with your OS's own voices.

  • ◉

    Remembers your chats

    Every conversation is saved to your own disk as readable JSON. Browse them from the panel, reopen any of them, and delete what you don't want — none of it is ever uploaded.

What “local” means here

Buddy has three separate jobs, and out of the box all three happen on your computer. You can move any of them to a cloud API, but nothing asks you to.

Job Default — no setup If you go looking
Thinking Llama 3.2 1B, inside the app Ollama · a cloud API
Talking your OS's own voices a cloud API
Hearing off — the mic is never opened local Whisper · a cloud API
Your chats always your disk never uploaded

The one exception is a single download on first launch: the model file, about 770 MB, comes from HuggingFace. After that, unplug your network and Buddy carries on working — please do check. There is no telemetry and no account.

If you do switch a job to a cloud API, that job stops being private, and we won't pretend otherwise: those messages go to the provider to be processed. What stays true either way is that there is no Buddy server, no proxy and no account — nothing of yours passes through us, because there is no “us” in the path.

About the wake word: Buddy spots speech locally, then transcribes a short clip to check whether you said “Hey Buddy”. Where that clip goes is exactly your hearing setting. Local Whisper means ambient audio never leaves your device. z-ai means short clips of ambient sound are sent there whenever something sounds like speech. Set hearing to off and Buddy never opens your microphone at all — you just type instead.

First launch

These builds aren't code-signed, so your OS will warn you the first time. That's expected — here's how to get past it:

  • Every OS: the first launch downloads Buddy's language model (~770 MB) and shows a progress bar. It resumes if interrupted. Nothing to configure.
  • macOS: right-click the app and choose Open (don't double-click). If it says the app is damaged, run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Buddy.app
  • Windows: on the SmartScreen prompt, click More info → Run anyway.
  • Linux: mark the AppImage executable — chmod +x Buddy-*.AppImage

What's inside

  • The draggable always-on-top orb, with its position remembered between launches
  • A Siri-style chat panel with spoken replies and a mic button
  • Saved conversations you can browse, reopen and delete — kept on your disk only
  • Its own language model, fetched once on first launch and run in-process with llama.cpp
  • “Hey Buddy” wake word, with a tray toggle to switch it off
  • A tray/menu-bar icon: open Buddy, toggle the wake word, quit
  • A loopback-only local server that holds your key and talks to whichever model you chose
